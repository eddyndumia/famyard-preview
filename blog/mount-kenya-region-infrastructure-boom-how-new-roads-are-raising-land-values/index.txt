20:Ta85,<p>Land value rarely moves on its own — it follows infrastructure. Across the Mount Kenya region, particularly Nyeri and Laikipia counties, road upgrades, improved connectivity, and expanding towns have been steadily reshaping which areas are worth watching for land investment.</p>
<h2>Why Roads Move Land Prices</h2>
<p>A tarmacked, all-weather road changes a plot’s usability year-round, not just its resale value. Areas that were once considered remote become commutable, farmable produce becomes easier to transport to market, and tourism-linked towns become easier to reach — all of which feed directly into demand for land along that corridor.</p>
<h2>What to Watch For</h2>
<p>When assessing whether a location’s infrastructure story supports a land investment, look at:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Road classification and maintenance plans</strong> — is it a road the county/national government has committed to upgrading, or an informal access track?</li>
<li><strong>Distance to a market center</strong> — proximity to towns like Karatina, Nyeri town, or Nanyuki tends to compound the effect of any given road upgrade.</li>
<li><strong>Utility rollout alongside roads</strong> — power and water infrastructure often follows the same corridors as road upgrades.</li>
<li><strong>Existing developer activity</strong> — serviced developments moving into an area are often an early signal that infrastructure investment is already underway or committed.</li>
</ul>
<h2>The Mount Kenya Region Specifically</h2>
<p>Towns across Nyeri and Laikipia — Nanyuki, Naru Moru, Karatina, Chaka and others — sit along corridors that benefit from both agricultural transport routes and tourism traffic heading toward Mount Kenya. That dual demand (farming and tourism) has historically made this region’s land market more resilient than areas dependent on a single economic driver.</p>
<h2>Investing With the Trend, Not Against It</h2>
<p>Buying land ahead of infrastructure completion is a recognized (if higher-risk) strategy, while buying just after a road is finished typically means paying a price that already reflects the improvement. Either way, verifying the actual state and government commitment behind a road — not just its condition on the day you visit — is essential. For related reading, see our <a href="/famyard-preview/blog/land-investment-ideas-that-will-pay-you-back-in-2025-and-beyond">land investment ideas guide</a>.</p>
